TL;DRRead time: 40s

AI is eliminating knowledge jobs faster than new roles are created and found. The economy will keep growing, but displaced people have no public infrastructure to find their way back into it. The Open Work Foundation exists to build that infrastructure: open, auditable, and free for the people who need it most.

01 /The problem

Every credible projection agrees on the direction: a large share of knowledge work will be automated or radically transformed this decade. Across moderate and extreme scenarios alike, three facts repeat:

↑
The economy keeps growing. GDP does not collapse, but income migrates from wages to capital. The machines' owners win; the displaced workforce does not automatically.
≠
The hit is asymmetric. Office and knowledge roles absorb the impact. Manual and in-person technical work may even gain value.
∄
The bottleneck is reallocation, not jobs. New roles will exist, but no infrastructure finds people and places them before a lost job becomes a lost year.

The danger is not technological unemployment as a concept. It is years of disorder: millions of capable people spending their savings, their confidence, and their skills in the gap between the job AI removed and the job nobody could point them to.

02 /What the experts project

We do not ask anyone to trust our framing. The scenario comes from the institutions that study labor markets for a living.

ILO · Generative AI and Jobs

A large share of global employment is exposed to generative AI, with knowledge-intensive roles at the highest exposure.

Whether exposure becomes substitution or augmentation is a policy choice, not a technology inevitability.

WEF · Future of Jobs Report

The churn is already measurable, not hypothetical.

Fastest-growing roles (AI, data, care, education) are rising while clerical and administrative roles decline; the reallocation has begun.

OECD · World Bank

Reallocation is the binding constraint on how painful the transition becomes.

Matching the displaced to emerging demand, not job creation alone, determines the human cost of the transition.

Industry research · labeled as such

Vendor scenario studies point the same direction.

Cited critically in Sources, never as the foundation of the argument.

03 /What the Foundation is for

Labor-market infrastructure today is private infrastructure. Job boards rank by revenue. Matching algorithms are black boxes. The displaced worker is the product being sold, not the person being served.

We steward the alternative.

  • 01

    Open protocols

    Standardized skills profiles and structured job descriptions any tool can read, not proprietary pipelines.

  • 02

    Auditable matching

    Transparent, typed judgments a person can inspect and challenge, not an ad-ranked feed.

  • 03

    Free for workers, always

    Funded by those who benefit from an orderly transition: employers, institutions, governments, philanthropy.

  • 04

    Steward, not owner

    Like the foundations behind the open-source commons: we host and govern projects; we do not own the labor market. MIT licensed. No one owns the idea.
