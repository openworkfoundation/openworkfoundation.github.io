## TL;DR

AI is eliminating knowledge work faster than new roles are created and found.
The economy will still grow — but the people being displaced have no public
infrastructure to find their way back into it. The Open Work Foundation exists
to build that infrastructure: open, auditable, and free for the people who
need it most.

## The problem

AI systems already perform tasks that were, until recently, the exclusive
domain of trained professionals: writing, analysis, coding, legal review,
customer operations. Every credible projection agrees on the direction: a
large share of knowledge work will be automated or radically transformed this
decade.

What the projections also show, across moderate and extreme scenarios alike:

- **The economy keeps growing.** GDP does not collapse — but income shifts
  from wages to capital. The machines' owners win; the displaced workforce
  does not automatically.
- **The hit is asymmetric.** Office and knowledge roles absorb the impact.
  Manual and in-person technical work may even gain value.
- **The bottleneck is reallocation, not jobs.** New roles will exist —
  but labor markets have no infrastructure that finds people and puts them
  there before a lost job becomes a lost year.

The danger is not technological unemployment as a concept. It is years of
disorder: millions of capable people spending their savings, their
confidence, and their skills in the gap between the job AI removed and the
job nobody could point them to.

## What the experts project

We do not ask anyone to trust our framing. The scenario comes from the
institutions that study labor markets for a living:

- The **ILO** projects that a large share of global employment is exposed to
  generative AI, with knowledge-intensive clerical and professional roles at
  the highest exposure — and that whether exposure becomes substitution or
  augmentation is a policy choice, not a technology inevitability.
- The **WEF Future of Jobs Report** tracks the fastest-growing roles
  (AI/data, care, education) against the fastest-declining ones — and shows
  the churn is already underway, not hypothetical.
- The **OECD** and **World Bank** document that reallocation — matching the
  displaced to emerging demand — is the binding constraint on how painful
  the transition becomes.

Full citations in [Sources](/sources). Where we cite industry research,
it is marked as such and read critically — vendors included.

## What the Foundation is for

Labor-market infrastructure today is private infrastructure. Job boards
rank by revenue. Matching algorithms are black boxes. The displaced worker
is the product being sold, not the person being served.

The Open Work Foundation exists to steward the alternative: public,
open-source, auditable infrastructure for the labor transition.

- **Open protocols**, not proprietary pipelines.
- **Auditable matching**, not ad-ranked feeds.
- **Free for workers, always.** Funding comes from those who benefit from an
  orderly transition: employers, institutions, governments, philanthropy.

We operate like the foundations behind the open-source commons: we host and
steward projects, we do not own the labor market and we do not sell anyone's
future.
