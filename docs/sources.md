# Sources

The evidence base this work stands on. Institutional sources are the
backbone; industry research is clearly labeled and read critically.

## Labor institutions

- **ILO — Generative AI and Jobs**: exposure analysis of occupations to
  generative AI; the augmentation-vs-substitution outcome is a policy
  choice. ilo.org
- **WEF — Future of Jobs Report**: tracks fastest-growing and
  fastest-declining roles globally; the churn is already measurable.
  weforum.org
- **OECD Employment Outlook**: AI, labor reallocation and the skills agenda.
  oecd.org
- **World Bank — World Development Report**: labor markets, technology and
  the future of work. worldbank.org

## Academic research

- Acemoglu & Restrepo — work on automation, displacement and the task
  content of jobs (NBER / journals).
- Autor, D. — labor-market polarization and new-work creation.

## Industry research (labeled as such)

- **Anthropic — Economic Scenarios for 2030**: scenario modeling where GDP
  grows but income shifts toward capital, with asymmetric impact on
  knowledge work. Vendor-produced; treated as one signal among many.
- **McKinsey Global Institute** — workforce transition and reskilling
  reports.
